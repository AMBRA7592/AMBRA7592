## Active

**TrialDiff** — deterministic, source-linked Evidence Records for
ClinicalTrials.gov protocol-amendment history. Each record is hash-pinned to the
patch that produced it, and event-class membership is decided by a published
rule-set rather than by judgement. Records assert the structural fact of a change
and nothing beyond it: no misconduct, no regulatory finding, no clinical
significance. Independent reconstructibility is reported, not assumed — the
current release states how many memberships are decidable from the embedded patch
alone and how many are not.

## Research

**Contractibility regimes and regulatory architecture.** When a novel financial
substrate outruns the categories built for prior asset classes, the usual move is
analogy — to insurance, securities, commodities, gambling. The alternative is to
decompose inherited regimes into primitive protection and hazard mechanisms and
recompose the bundle the new substrate actually needs. Event-contract markets are
the live specimen, and the CFTC's 2026 rulemaking on prediction markets is the
occasion.

A related line asks what institutions can admit, govern, and revoke — externally
maintained software artifacts, external state that outlives its support, and
claims that keep being priced after the relation producing them has ended.

## Maintained

**[Criticality Spectrometer](https://github.com/AMBRA7592/criticality-spectrometer)**
— node criticality is a curve, not a score. Removes each node, sweeps the horizon
at which substitutes appear, and records mission loss at every horizon. Runs
unchanged across an AI-compute supply chain and a Kubernetes service mesh; the
answer moves with the *view*, not the code. Two bounded domains are a transfer
test, not a proof of general applicability, and the repository states its
[non-claims](https://github.com/AMBRA7592/criticality-spectrometer/blob/v0.2.0/docs/nonclaims.md)
explicitly.
[PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/criticality-spectrometer/) ·
[DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21383918](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21383918)

**[Who Holds the State?](https://github.com/AMBRA7592/who-holds-the-state)** — a
framework for testing claims after their support ends, with a twelve-row launch
registry applying it across clinical, financial, and household cases.
